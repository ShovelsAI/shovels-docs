**Shovels collects permit data directly from city and county jurisdictions—we don't purchase data from third-party vendors.** We gather data through relationships with local governments, integration with online permitting portals, and records requested from the jurisdiction directly. This direct sourcing ensures data quality and freshness.

## Primary Collection Methods

### Direct from Jurisdictions

We collect data through:
- **Relationships with local governments**
- **Integration with online permitting portals**
- **Records requested from the jurisdiction** where permits aren't published online

### Digital Methods
- Jurisdiction website scraping
- Municipal open data portal integration
- Building department API connections

### Permits Not Published Online

Thousands of jurisdictions issue building permits without making them publicly available online. Some have no permit portal at all; others keep their records in a system the public can't search. Either way the data has to come from the jurisdiction directly.

For each of these jurisdictions we:

1. **Confirm the records aren't already online.** If they are, they go through our online pipeline instead.
2. **Find the right office and its procedure.** Some offices take an informal request straight to the building department. Others require a formal public records request under their state's own law. The route differs for every jurisdiction.
3. **Set up a recurring delivery.** We agree with the office on how records will be sent and whether a fee applies. Requests go out by email, form, records portal, or occasionally fax. Some jurisdictions simply add us to a distribution list and send on their own schedule, with no request needed.
4. **Review each delivery.** Records arrive by email or are posted to a portal, as spreadsheets, PDFs, and occasionally paper. We check every delivery against what we asked for and follow up on anything missing.
5. **Standardize and check.** Each jurisdiction's records are mapped to the Shovels schema and put through quality checks before publication.

Most of these jurisdictions send new records monthly, though the cadence ranges from weekly to twice a year. A permit typically reaches the dataset about a month after it is issued.

These permits carry the same schema as every other permit and appear in the same searches, exports, and API responses. Most include a permit number, address, status, and description; permit type, valuation, contractor, and parcel details are included where the jurisdiction provides them. Field completeness varies by jurisdiction.

## Supplementary Sources

### Address Standardization
We cross-reference addresses against:
- National Address Dataset (US Census)
- Open Address dataset
- Simple Maps
- ESRI

### Contractor Verification
We match contractor information against:
- Publicly available state license files
- Business registration records

### Property Information
We enhance property data using:
- Tax assessor records

## Why Direct Sourcing Matters

By collecting directly from jurisdictions, we:
- Get the most current data available
- Control data quality from the start
- Standardize across different formats
- Maintain consistent update schedules
